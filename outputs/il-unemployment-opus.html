Israel unemployment rate (SA, CBS LFS) — next published month

מה יהיה שיעור האבטלה בישראל בחודש הבא? (What will Israel's unemployment rate be next month?)

3.1% for August 2026 (90% interval 2.7-3.5); 3.1% for September 2026 (90% interval 2.6-3.6) - from the random-walk benchmark, not from Google Trends

The next CBS print (August 2026, due mid-September) is forecast at 3.1%, unchanged from July's 3.1%, with a 90% rolling-conformal interval of 2.7-3.5 pp; September 2026 is also 3.1% (2.6-3.6). The number comes from the target-only 'naive' (random-walk) benchmark, which won the rolling-origin backtest at every horizon: the best Google-Trends model (ridge on rolling-detrended Hebrew search series, no clustering) scored OOS R2 +0.008 vs naive at h=0 (Clark-West p=0.078, DM p=0.89, n=115 origins), +0.018 at h=1 (CW p=0.041, DM p=0.78, n=114) and -0.030 at h=2 (lasso+raw, CW p=0.33, n=113) - no reliable gain. Excluding the COVID/furlough window 2020-03..2021-06 the same GT model is 'moderate' (+0.031 / +0.041 / +0.086 OOS R2, CW p 0.035 / 0.023 / 0.019, but DM p 0.45-0.55, n=97-99): suggestive, not validated, and it can only lower confidence, never raise it. Confidence: low - the answer is the benchmark's. The Aug-2026 figure is a genuine nowcast (monthly Google Trends already covers August; the last CBS print is July). For reference the GT model itself would say 3.06% (Aug) and 2.94% (Sep) - within rounding of the random walk, with standardised coefficients below 0.02. Israeli layoff-related search interest is elevated (piturim 77 in August vs a 46 trailing-12-month mean, peaking at 100 in June) but benefit-claim searches (dmey avtala, bituach leumi avtala) are flat and the backtest says these terms do not improve on the random walk, so the elevated layoff attention is reported, not forecast. Google Trends measures relative search attention, not counts. Caveats: CBS LFS seasonally-adjusted rate, ages 15+, spliced at 2025-01 from the old (2008-census) to the new (2022-census) definition (mean gap 0.04 pp, max 0.1 pp over the 12 overlapping months of 2025); monthly survey noise is about +/-0.2 pp, which is most of the interval width; in 2020-21 furloughed workers were counted as employed, so the headline rate barely moved while search interest exploded - that mismatch is why raw-GT models look worst in the full sample; the regime split (median 4.2%) shows the benchmark beating the GT model in both the low regime (MAE 0.164 vs 0.171, which is where the rate is now at 3.1%) and the high regime (0.171 vs 0.179).

Method

  • Target frequency M, 175 periods 2012-01 → 2026-07; Google Trends pulled individually per query (geo=IL), cached as dated vintages.
  • Rolling-origin backtest (120 origins max, min_train=60), direct multi-step design, differences; preprocessing fitted inside each training window.
  • Benchmark = best target-only model among naive / seasonal naive / AR: naive. GT models: ridge+raw, lasso+raw, ridge+djorno, ridge+djorno_nocluster, lasso+djorno.
  • Verdict rule: Google Trends 'helps' only if OOS R² > 0.02 vs the benchmark and Clark–West or Diebold–Mariano p < 0.10.
  • Interval: rolling conformal from the last ≤60 out-of-sample residuals of the chosen model (nominal 90%).

Caveats

  • Google Trends values are relative search interest (0–100 per request), sampled and revised by Google; not counts.
  • Search attention reacts to news as well as to the underlying quantity; relationships drift (ARGO-style rolling refits mitigate, not eliminate).
  • Out-of-sample gains concentrated in shock periods do not guarantee gains in calm periods — see the robustness table.
