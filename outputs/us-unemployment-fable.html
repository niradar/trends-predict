Method

  • Target frequency M, 272 periods 2004-01 → 2026-08; Google Trends pulled individually per query (geo=US), cached as dated vintages.
  • Rolling-origin backtest (120 origins max, min_train=60), direct multi-step design, differences; preprocessing fitted inside each training window.
  • Benchmark = best target-only model among naive / seasonal naive / AR: naive. GT models: ridge+raw, lasso+raw, ridge+djorno, ridge+djorno_nocluster, lasso+djorno.
  • Verdict rule: Google Trends 'helps' only if OOS R² > 0.02 vs the benchmark and Clark–West or Diebold–Mariano p < 0.10.
  • Interval: rolling conformal from the last ≤60 out-of-sample residuals of the chosen model (nominal 90%).

Caveats

  • Google Trends values are relative search interest (0-100 per request), sampled and revised by Google; not counts.
  • Both horizons are one-month-ahead forecasts from an August-2026 origin: Google Trends monthly data end in 2026-08, so no true nowcast of September was possible (the h=0 row is not a nowcast).
  • The shock-excluded gain comes from 2020 being removed; in the full sample the GT models were hurt by the April-2020 jump (regime split: benchmark MAE 1.19 vs GT 1.39 when unemployment > 5 %). Gains in calm periods are 0.01-0.03 pp of MAE - below the 0.1 pp rounding of the BLS print.
  • October 2025 is missing from BLS (government shutdown) and was interpolated for alignment.
  • The 90 % interval (+/-0.2 pp) is a rolling conformal interval from the last <=60 out-of-sample naive residuals; it does not cover a 2020-type shock.
