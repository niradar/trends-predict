Will the S&P 500 be higher a month from now?

About a 60 % chance the S&P 500 is higher in a month - the historical base rate; Google Trends adds no measurable edge

Probably, but only in the weak sense that stocks usually rise: the validated answer is the target-only benchmark, not Google Trends. Point forecast (drift benchmark, i.e. random walk with drift): October 2026 monthly close 7,796, 90 % rolling-conformal interval 7,058–8,611, from the last complete month (August 2026 close 7,686). For the September 2026 close the point is 7,741 (90 % interval 7,146–8,385). Implied probability of being higher: about 60 % over two months and 57 % over one — essentially the unconditional base rate (66 % of months and 73 % of two-month spans since 2004 were up). Evidence: benchmark = drift (it beat naive, AR and seasonal-naive). Best Google-Trends model at h=1 was ridge+diffgt with OOS R² = -0.01 vs drift (Clark-West p = 0.35, Diebold-Mariano p = 0.72, 120 rolling origins); at h=2 ridge+diffgt reached OOS R² = +0.01 (CW p = 0.13, DM p = 0.49, n = 120) - not significant. Excluding the Feb-Jun 2020 crash the best models gain +0.002 (h=1, lasso+raw, n = 115) and +0.009 (h=2), both with CW p = 0.18. Directional accuracy: drift 67.5 % (h=1) and 71.7 % (h=2); no Google-Trends model beat it. Verdict: none, in both the full-sample and the shock-excluded views. Confidence: low. What the search data does show (description, not forecast): attention is coincident, not leading. "bear market", "stock market" and "vix" correlate -0.40, -0.34 and -0.34 with the same month's return but only -0.00, -0.11 and -0.09 with the next month's. Right now risk-attention is mildly elevated (vix +22 %, "bear market" +18 % over the last three months vs the previous twelve) while buy-side terms are down ("stocks to buy" -26 %, "buy stocks" -20 %) - suggestive of caution, but with no validated predictive value at a one-month horizon. Caveats: the anchor is the August close (7,686); the index traded near 7,640-7,660 in mid-September, so the forecast is only about +2 % above the current level - inside the noise. Monthly Google Trends data end with the last complete month, so there is no true nowcast here. Google Trends values are relative search attention, not counts. The interval is a rolling conformal interval calibrated on out-of-sample residuals and is wide (about ±10 %) because monthly equity returns are close to unpredictable.

Method

  • Target frequency M, 272 periods 2004-01 → 2026-08; Google Trends pulled individually per query (geo=US), cached as dated vintages.
  • Rolling-origin backtest (120 origins max, min_train=60), direct multi-step design, differences; preprocessing fitted inside each training window.
  • Benchmark = best target-only model among naive / seasonal naive / AR: drift. GT models: ridge+raw, ols+raw, lasso+raw, ridge+djorno, ridge+djorno_nocluster, lasso+djorno, ridge+diffgt.
  • Verdict rule: Google Trends 'helps' only if OOS R² > 0.02 vs the benchmark and Clark–West or Diebold–Mariano p < 0.10.
  • Interval: rolling conformal from the last ≤60 out-of-sample residuals of the chosen model (nominal 90%).

Caveats

  • Google Trends values are relative search interest (0–100 per request), sampled and revised by Google; not counts.
  • Search attention reacts to news as well as to the underlying quantity; relationships drift (ARGO-style rolling refits mitigate, not eliminate).
  • Out-of-sample gains concentrated in shock periods do not guarantee gains in calm periods — see the robustness table.
