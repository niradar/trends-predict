# Experiment 01-Q1-B — US unemployment rate, ARGO-style (LASSO / elastic-net on expanded queries)

Date: 2026-09-17 · Script: `research/experiments/scripts/01-Q1-B.py` · Data: `research/experiments/01-Q1-B.json`
GT requests: **26** (3 `related_queries` + 23 new individual pulls; 4 terms were already cached) · Cached rerun: 222 s

## 1. Setup

- **Question (Q1):** What will the US unemployment rate be next month?
- **Truth:** BLS `LNS14000000` (seasonally adjusted unemployment rate) via `truth.bls_series("UNRATE")`,
  monthly, 2004-01 → 2026-08 (271 obs). Last published value: **4.1 % (Aug 2026)**.
- **Queries — generation:** seeds `["unemployment", "unemployment benefits", "jobs"]` →
  `gt.expand_queries(seeds, geo="US", timeframe=gt.timeframe_for("M"), max_per_seed=12)` → 35 unique terms.
  **8 dropped** (27 kept, within the 20–40 target):

  | dropped term | reason |
  |---|---|
  | texas benefits | not unemployment-specific (Texas HHS portal: SNAP/Medicaid) |
  | job | single generic word; dominated by unrelated meanings (Steve Jobs 2011, "job" as task) |
  | jobs indeed | word-order duplicate of `indeed` (kept) |
  | ny unemployment / nj unemployment | word-order duplicates of `unemployment ny` / `unemployment nj` (kept) |
  | craigslist jobs | platform-branded; secular decline of Craigslist dominates the series |
  | amazon jobs | single-employer term; tracks Amazon's growth, not the labour market |
  | usa jobs | federal hiring portal (usajobs.gov); government-hiring specific |

  **27 kept:** unemployment, unemployment benefits, unemployment login, unemployment claim, unemployment rate,
  unemployment nj, unemployment number, ohio unemployment, unemployment pa, unemployment ny, unemployment office,
  texas unemployment, unemployment texas benefits, claim unemployment benefits, ny unemployment benefits,
  unemployment benefits login, unemployment benefits extended, new unemployment benefits, unemployment benefits
  extension, jobs, jobs near me, indeed, jobs hiring, state jobs, part time jobs, remote jobs, jobs from home.
- **Pull:** `gt.fetch_many(terms, geo="US", timeframe="2004-01-01 2026-09-17", mode="individual")` — one request per
  term, monthly, 2004-01 → 2026-08 (the partial Sep-2026 row is dropped by the library). Privacy-zero filter dropped
  none; no failed pulls (several 429 back-offs, all recovered).
- **Alignment:** `truth.align(y, X, "M")` → 271 common months.
- **Design:** `transform="diff"` (model the change from the last known level), AR p=3 in differences, GT lags 0–1
  (`xlags=1`) → 54 GT columns + 3 AR + 2 Fourier (period 12, k=1) = 59 features. Preprocessing
  `pp.raw_pipeline` (ZeroRepair → log1p) fitted inside each training window.
- **Models:** `lasso` (LassoCV, time-series CV) and `enet` (ElasticNetCV, l1_ratio ∈ {0.2,0.5,0.8,0.95}), each with
  (a) expanding window and (b) sliding `window=36`. Benchmarks: naive (random walk), seasonal naive, AR-diff.
- **Protocol:** rolling origin, `min_train=60`, `max_origins=120` (targets 2016-09 → 2026-08), refit every origin.
  Horizons: **h=0 nowcast** (`y_known_at_origin=False`: latest month's rate unknown, its GT known) and **h=1**
  (`y_known_at_origin=True`). Benchmark for `ev.compare` = the best target-only model by RMSE = **naive** at both
  horizons (AR-diff is much worse with 2020 in sample and still 17 % worse without it).

## 5. Surprises / problems

1. **The 2020 turning point was *not* caught by the nowcast.** 62 % of the full-sample squared error of
   `lasso_exp+GT` (h=0) comes from one month: at origin 2020-04, with April GT already known (all claim/login terms
   at all-time highs), the model predicted 4.7 % against 14.8 %. Trained on 2004–2020-03 in log-levels, it had never
   seen inputs that far out of range and the penalised coefficients could not extrapolate. It then overshot in
   2020-08 (15.1 % vs 8.4 %). So the ARGO framing "GT shines at turning points" is not reproduced here.
2. **Non-reproducibility of 00b.** Re-running the 00b configuration (10 hand-picked terms, lasso + raw GT, h=0)
   with today's library gives RMSE 1.605 (excl. 2020: 0.312), not the 0.442 / 0.243 reported in
   `00b-smoke-unemployment.md`. Same cache, same settings; likely a library change between 19:38 and 19:40 (the
   evaluate/models modules were edited after the smoke script ran) or the CV alpha grid. Worth pinning before
   `research/conclusions.md` compares cells. Script: `scratch/diag_01B.py`.
3. **More queries hurt, not helped.** Against the 10-term smoke set run under identical current code
   (RMSE 1.605 / 0.312), the 27-term ARGO set is better in the full sample (1.164) and slightly better excluding
   2020 (0.225) — but both are far behind naive (1.015 / 0.139). Expansion adds ~15 near-duplicate state
   portal / login terms that co-move with `unemployment`; LASSO spreads weight across them rather than gaining
   information.
4. **Sliding window = 36 rows vs 59 columns.** Inner CV mostly selects the null model; when it does select
   (2–4 features), the resulting predictions are volatile (`lasso_w36` h=0 full-sample RMSE 3.08 — worst of all).
   36 months is too short for this feature count; ARGO's 104-week window on weekly data has ~3× the rows.
5. **Clark–West ≠ better.** `lasso_exp+GT`/`enet_exp+GT` have CW p = 0.004 at h=0 excl. 2020 while their OOS R² is
   −1.6 / −1.3 and DM says significantly worse. Seasonal naive gets CW p = 0.057 with R² = −4.5. The verdict rule's
   "R² > 0 AND CW/DM" conjunction is what prevents a false positive here.
6. **Shared quota.** Other cells were pulling concurrently (election, flu terms appeared in the catalog during my
   run) and the client logged repeated 429 warnings; the library's back-off recovered every time and the 23 live
   pulls still completed in 28 s (catalog timestamps 16:47:45 → 16:48:13 UTC). Counting requests by
   `len(gt.catalog())` before/after is unreliable in a shared cache (it grew by 5 during my `related_queries`
   call from other cells' pulls) — I counted only slugs matching my own keywords.
7. `DataFrame.to_markdown` needs `tabulate`, which is not installed; the script formats markdown by hand.
8. No leakage found: `y_known_at_origin=False` at h=0 removes the origin month's rate; the h=1 base is the origin
   month's known level; preprocessing is fitted inside the window; the truth is the current BLS vintage (revisions
   to the SA rate are small but this is *not* a real-time vintage evaluation).

## 7. Recommendation for the skill

For a persistent monthly macro series like the unemployment rate, the ARGO recipe (seed → `related_queries`
expansion → 25–40 raw log-GT terms → LASSO/elastic-net ARX) should **not** be the default: it never beats the
random walk here, the sliding 36-month window is under-determined for that many columns, and the expanding
window over-selects at h=1. The skill should (i) always report the naive random walk as the bar and say plainly
when GT does not clear it, (ii) if it uses query expansion, cap the kept set at ~10–15 terms after dropping
state-portal / login / branded duplicates or cluster them (approach C), (iii) prefer h=0 nowcast framing only when
there is a complete unpublished GT month — otherwise say the "next month" answer is the h=1 forecast, (iv) present
the point as "unchanged at ≈4.1 % with a ±0.25 pp 90 % band" rather than dressing the random walk up as a
GT model, and (v) refuse to claim GT catches turning points unless the backtest shows it at the actual shock
months (here it did not — the April-2020 miss was 10 pp).
