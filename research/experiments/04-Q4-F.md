# Experiment 04-Q4-F — "Who will win the next US election?" via Google Trends attention share

Date: 2026-09-17 · Script: `research/experiments/scripts/04-Q4-F.py` · Runtime 115 s (first run; re-runs from cache < 1 s) · GT requests: 56 new (budget 60)

Approach F: use the joint-normalised search share of candidate A vs B (or party vs party) as a proxy
for a discrete outcome, **validated on history first**. No paper does this; the goal was to measure
honestly whether the share has any out-of-sample predictive value before touching 2026.

## 1. Setup

- **Question.** Today is 2026-09-17; the next US election is the 2026 midterms (2026-11-03). Concrete
  target: which party wins the national House popular vote (proxy for House control). Presidential
  contests 2008–2024 and midterms 2006–2022 are the validation set.
- **Truth (hard-coded in the script, source: Wikipedia election pages / FEC certified totals).**
  Popular-vote margin, + = Democrat: 2008 +7.27, 2012 +3.86, 2016 +2.09 (EC → R), 2020 +4.45, 2024 −1.48.
  Swing-state margins 2016/2020/2024 for PA, MI, WI, GA, AZ, NV, NC (Wikipedia state pages).
  House popular vote: 2006 D+8.0, 2010 R+6.8, 2014 R+5.7, 2018 D+8.6, 2022 R+2.8.
- **Queries.** All pulls are *joint* 2-keyword `gt.fetch_interest` requests (one 0–100 scale per pair),
  `geo="US"` or `US-<state>`, explicit windows "YYYY-06-01 <election day>". Google returns **daily**
  resolution for these ~5-month windows (155–161 rows), not weekly as assumed in the plan.
  - Step 1: candidate names (5 pairs; 4 were the earlier probe pulls, reused from cache in the same keyword order).
  - Step 2: four intent modifiers per pair: `vote for {name}`, `{name} rally`, `{name} policies`, `{name} for president` (20 pulls).
  - Step 3: name pairs in 7 swing states × 2016/2020/2024 (21 pulls).
  - Step 4: `["Democratic Party","Republican Party"]` and `["vote Democrat","vote Republican"]` for 5 midterms (10 pulls).
  - Step 5: the same two party pairs for `2026-06-01 2026-09-17` (2 pulls).
  - Plus 2 forced same-day re-pulls (2014, 2018 party names) for a retrieval-noise check. 0 pulls failed; nothing dropped.
- **Statistics.** `share_last4` = Dem-side sum / (Dem + Rep) over the 28 days ending the day *before*
  election day; `trend_4v4` = share(last 28 d) − share(previous 28 d); `slope_8w` = OLS slope of weekly
  share over the last 8 weeks; `share_full` = whole window. "Hit" = (share > 0.5) agrees with the sign
  of the margin. Zero-day share = fraction of days on which at least one side is 0 (sparsity indicator).
- **Pre-registered validation rule** (added after the first run exposed that a naive rule — "≥80 % hits and
  r > 0.5" — would fire on n = 5): a relationship is validated only if n ≥ 8, exact binomial p < 0.05 for
  the hit-rate, and Pearson p < 0.05 for share vs margin, and it survives leave-one-out thresholding
  and a majority-class baseline.

Hit-rate vs popular vote **2/5**; vs Electoral College 3/5. Pearson(share, margin) = **−0.21**

`trend_4v4` was positive for the Democrat in all five years (4/5 "hits" only because 4 of 5 margins were D+) — a
constant, not a signal.

| relationship | n | hits | binomial p | corr p | passes rule? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| presidential raw share vs popular vote | 5 | 2 | 0.81 | 0.73 | no |
| swing-state share vs state winner | 21 | 12 | 0.33 | 0.23 | no |
| midterm party-name share vs House margin | 5 | 4 | 0.19 | 0.072 | no (n < 8; suggestive only) |
| midterm "vote party" share vs House margin | 5 | 3 | 0.50 | 0.67 | no |
| **pooled history** | **36** | **21 (58 %)** | | | **no** |

One-sentence reading: **Google Trends attention share does not predict election winners — 21/36 pooled
hits (58 %), presidential 2/5, states 12/21, and the one suggestive pair (midterm party names, 4/5,
r = 0.84) has n = 5, a 0.07 dynamic range, a leave-one-out call decided by 0.001, and p ≥ 0.07.**

## 4. Forward forecast

**None issued (deliberate refusal).** No relationship met the pre-registered validation rule, so
the deliverable is: "Google Trends attention share does not predict winners: hit-rate 21/36 pooled,
2/5 presidential." For transparency the current unvalidated reading of the only suggestive pair is:
party-name Dem share (last 4 weeks) 0.456 vs historical midterm mean 0.485, i.e. nominally R-leaning —
but 0.535 (D-leaning) once the single 2026-09-05 spike week is removed. A number that flips sign on one
week of news is not a forecast and is not reported as one.

## 5. Surprises / problems

- **Resolution.** Google returns daily data for ≤ ~9-month windows; "last 4 weeks" therefore means 28 daily
  values, and integer rounding on a 0–100 joint scale makes low-volume sides (the modifiers) mostly zeros.
- **Direction inversion.** Raw and modified shares correlate *negatively* with the margin in every
  presidential variant: the challenger / novel / controversial candidate is searched more (Romney 2012,
  Trump 2016, Harris 2024 after the July switch). This is the opposite of a support signal and would make
  a naive "more searches = more votes" skill actively wrong.
- **Naive validation rule almost passed a coin-flip.** The first version of the script declared the
  midterm party-name pair "validated" on 4/5 hits and r > 0.5. Tightening to a pre-registered rule with
  n ≥ 8 and p-values reversed that. Recorded here because the same trap awaits the skill.
- **2024 pair includes the pre-Harris period** (window starts 2024-06-01, Harris became candidate
  2024-07-21); last-4/8-week statistics are unaffected, `share_full` is not comparable for 2024.
- **Retrieval noise could not be measured** within one day (identical samples). Shares that differ by
  0.01–0.02 between years should be assumed to be inside the noise band until a cross-day check exists.
- **Shared catalog.** Other cells pulled concurrently (catalog grew 17 → 169 during this cell); request
  attribution was done by matching this cell's request signatures, not by catalog delta.
- No `ev.compare` / rolling-origin backtest applies here (discrete outcomes, n = 5–21); hit-rates with
  exact binomial p-values and correlations with p-values were used instead, as stated in the cell brief.

## 7. Recommendation for the skill

For "who will win <election>" questions the skill should **not** produce a Google-Trends-based winner
call. It should say explicitly that search attention share has no validated relationship with election
outcomes (this cell: 2/5 presidential, 12/21 swing-state, 21/36 pooled; every presidential variant is
negatively correlated with the margin because attention tracks novelty and controversy, not support),
and that party-level terms for midterms are suggestive at best (4/5, n = 5, dynamic range 0.07, one call
decided by 0.001). What the skill *can* honestly offer: (a) show the attention share and its weekly
history as a *salience* indicator with a caveat that it is not a support measure; (b) redirect the user
to validated sources — polling averages, prediction markets, structural forecasts — and, if the user
wants Trends in the loop at all, reframe the target as something continuous and measurable
(e.g. nowcasting turnout or a polling average from queries like "polling place"/"register to vote",
which would need its own validation cell). If a discrete call is unavoidable, the skill must state the
historical hit-rate with n next to it, never present a share > 0.5 as a probability, and never issue a
number that flips sign when one news-spike week is removed, as the 2026 series does today.
