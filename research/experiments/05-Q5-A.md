# Experiment 05-Q5-A — Israel unemployment next month, Approach A (Choi–Varian minimal, Hebrew)

Date: 2026-09-17 · Script: `research/experiments/scripts/05-Q5-A.py` · Runtime 10 s (cached GT) · GT interest pulls: 9 (+2 suggestion calls)
Results: `research/experiments/05-Q5-A.json`

Question (Hebrew): "מה יהיה שיעור האבטלה בישראל בחודש הבא?" — What will Israel's unemployment rate be next month?

## 1. Setup

**Truth series** — CBS (Israel Central Bureau of Statistics) Labour Force Survey, unemployment rate, ages 15+,
total population, **seasonally adjusted**, % of civilian labour force, monthly, 2012-01 → 2026-07 (175 obs).
Keyless, via the CBS series API (documented here because the library lacked it; saved with
`truth._save("cbs_unemployment_rate_il", ...)` → `data/truth/cbs_unemployment_rate_il.csv` + `.meta.json`):

- Catalog navigation: `GET https://apis.cbs.gov.il/series/catalog/level?id=1&format=json` (subjects) →
  subject 11 = "נתוני תעסוקה על פי סקר כוח אדם" (LFS employment data) →
  `catalog/level?id=2&subject=11` → sub-catalogs [11,5] "Jan-2012..Dec-2025, old definition, monthly LFS (2008 census)"
  and [11,6] "from Jan-2025, new definition, monthly LFS (2022 census)". Leaf listing: `catalog/path?id=11,5` (7,111 leaves,
  paged 100/page). Series metadata (level-4 variable name, original/SA/trend variant) is only exposed by the data
  endpoint, so I enumerated `data/path?id=11,{5|6},1,{L4},2226` and selected level-4 = "בלתי מועסקים" (unemployed),
  unit = "אחוזים" (%), data = "מנוכי עונתיות" (seasonally adjusted).
- Data download: `GET https://apis.cbs.gov.il/series/data/list?id={sid}&format=json&download=false&PageSize=1000`
  - **sid 491094** (old definition, SA, %, 2012-01..2025-12, `update 2026-01-20`) — used for 2012-01..2024-12
  - **sid 41097** (new definition, SA, %, 2025-01..2026-07, `update 2026-08-16`) — used for 2025-01..2026-07
  - Non-SA ("נתונים מקוריים") counterparts are sid 490013 / 40013; trend ("נתוני מגמה") 491095 / 41098.
- Splice at 2025-01. Over the 12 overlapping months of 2025 the new-definition series is on average 0.04 pp
  higher than the old one (max |diff| = 0.1 pp), so the series were spliced without adjustment (break documented in meta).
- Range 2.6 % (2024-08) to 7.2 % (2012-03); mean since 2022 is 3.25 %. The COVID furlough period raised the SA
  rate only to 4.2–5.4 % (2020-05..2021-08) because furloughed workers (חל"ת) were counted as employed, temporarily absent.
- Latest print: 2026-07 = 3.1 % (published 2026-08-16). GT extends to 2026-08, so a genuine h=0 nowcast of August 2026 exists.

**Google Trends queries** — hand-picked Hebrew terms, `geo="IL"`, monthly (`2004-01-01 <today>`), individual pulls

Privacy zeros are a 2004–2011 problem only; since 2012 (the sample actually used) every kept Hebrew series is 100 % non-zero.

**Design** — aligned monthly sample 2012-01..2026-07 (175 months); expanding window; `transform="diff"`; p=3 AR lags;
GT lags 0–1 (`xlags=1`); `period=12`, `fourier_k=1`; `min_train=48` (per cell) → 127 rolling origins at h=0
(2016-01..2026-07), 126 at h=1. Horizons: h=0 nowcast (`y_known_at_origin=False`) and h=1 (`y_known_at_origin=True`).
Views: full sample and `exclude=("2020-03-01","2021-06-30")` (COVID furlough scheme).
Models: `ols+rawGT` (OLS ARX, `pp.raw_pipeline`), `ridge+djorno` (ridge, `pp.djorno_pipeline(period=12, halflife=1.0)`),
benchmarks {naive, seasonal_naive, ar_diff}. Bench per view = lowest-RMSE target-only model in that view.

Honest reading: the Israeli SA unemployment rate since 2012 is a slow random walk with ±0.2 pp monthly noise; the
random walk is the best model at both horizons, `ridge+djorno` merely ties it (RMSE 0.215 vs 0.214), and the raw-GT
OLS model is *significantly worse* (DM p < 0.01 at h=0, p = 0.03 at h=1 excl.) — the Hebrew queries add estimation
noise, not signal.

## 4. Forward forecast

- **Winner (both horizons): naive.** Origin 2026-07 (last CBS print, 3.1 %), target **2026-08**.
- **Point 3.1 %, 90 % conformal interval [2.7, 3.5]** (half-width 0.40 pp from the last 36 backtest errors; identical
  using all origins). Rolling empirical coverage of the same rule in the backtest: 91 % (h=0), 91 % (h=1).
- GT-model reference numbers for August 2026 (not the headline): `ridge+djorno` h=0 nowcast 3.07 [2.70, 3.43];
  `ols+rawGT` h=0 3.02 [2.64, 3.40]; at h=1 both give 3.07–3.08. Largest ridge coefficients: the cluster
  `דמי אבטלה+5` (benefits / employment-service terms merged by the Djorno clustering) and `דרושים` (job ads, negative
  sign at lag 1); all coefficients are tiny (|β| < 0.02 on the standardised scale), i.e. the model is effectively the random walk.
- Note: the h=0 nowcast really does use August 2026 search data (X extends one period beyond y); the answer to the
  user's question is nevertheless "about the same as last month".

## 5. Surprises / problems

1. **Gershayim break Google Trends.** `חל"ת` (the standard Hebrew abbreviation for furlough) is sent verbatim, but Google
   treats `"` as a phrase operator and strips it; the returned column is literally `חל ת` — a nonsense two-token query
   with a 4 % non-zero share, correctly dropped by the privacy-zero filter. The quote-less spelling `חלת` works
   (85 % non-zero share since 2004, 100 % since 2012). The skill must strip `"`/`״` from Hebrew abbreviations (or expand
   them) before querying, and should detect the column-name mismatch in `_finish` as a warning.
2. **Privacy zeros were not the problem I expected.** All six "real" Hebrew terms are ≥ 90 % non-zero even from 2004
   and 100 % from 2012; monthly resolution over 22 years is coarse enough for Israeli search volumes.
3. **CBS series API works but is under-documented.** The `catalog/level` endpoint ignores sub-subject parameters
   beyond level 2 (it returns level-3 nodes for *all* level-2 branches), and the leaf listing (`catalog/path`) carries only
   the population-group name, so identifying the variable/variant requires calling the data endpoint for each level-4
   node. The old-definition branch ends 2025-12 and the 2025+ branch has a different series id; the splice is smooth
   (≤ 0.1 pp). Also, the `data/path` response pages at 100 observations — use `data/list?id=<sid>&PageSize=1000`.
4. **The COVID exclusion barely matters** because the target itself did not spike: the Israeli furlough scheme kept
   the SA unemployment rate at ≤ 5.4 %. Search interest in חל"ת/דמי אבטלה did explode in 2020, which is exactly why
   the raw-GT OLS model is worse in the full sample — it learned a level relationship that never showed in the target.
   A "broad unemployment" measure (unemployed + temporarily absent for economic reasons) would be the fairer target
   for 2020–21; CBS publishes it, but it is a different series and out of scope for this cell.
5. **Request counting under parallel agents.** `len(gt.catalog())` before/after jumped by 78 during my 7-pull probe run
   because other experiment cells were appending to the same `data/trends/catalog.jsonl`. `gt.REQUEST_COUNT`
   (process-local) is the reliable counter; the JSON reports both.
6. Trendspy emitted three 429 rate-limit warnings during the probe pulls; the library backoff recovered every time
   (`info["failed"]` empty). Total wall time of the probe run was ~2 min; the final script runs in 10 s from cache.

## 7. Recommendation for the skill

For "what will Israel's unemployment rate be next month" the skill should (a) fetch the CBS LFS SA rate via
`apis.cbs.gov.il/series/data/list?id=41097` (new definition, 2025-) spliced onto `id=491094` (2012–2024) and state
clearly which definition and that it is seasonally adjusted; (b) answer with the random walk — "same as the last
published month, ≈3.1 %, 90 % band ±0.4 pp" — and explicitly say that Hebrew Google Trends terms (benefits,
employment service, layoffs, job ads, furlough, and the language-neutral Unemployment topic) do **not** improve on that
at h=0 or h=1 in a 2016–2026 backtest, so it refuses to dress the answer up as a GT-based forecast; (c) sanitise Hebrew
abbreviations with gershayim (`חל"ת` → `חלת`) before querying and warn when Google returns a renamed column;
(d) caveat that during furlough-type shocks the headline SA rate understates labour-market stress, so a search-volume
spike may be real even though the target does not move — offer the broad-unemployment series instead of claiming GT
"failed". Privacy zeros are not a blocker for Hebrew monthly series since 2012, so the skill need not fall back to
English or topic ids for Israel unless the term is very niche.
