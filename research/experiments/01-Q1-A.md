# Experiment 01-Q1-A — US unemployment next month, Approach A (Choi–Varian minimal)

Date: 2026-09-17 · Script: `research/experiments/scripts/01-Q1-A.py` · Data: `01-Q1-A.json` · Runtime 6 s · GT requests: 1 new (4 of 5 keywords were already cached)

## 1. Setup

- **Question (Q1):** What will the US unemployment rate be next month?
- **Truth:** BLS `LNS14000000` (seasonally adjusted unemployment rate, %) via `truth.bls_series("UNRATE")`,
  monthly, 2004-01 → 2026-08, 271 observations. **2025-10 is missing** (the household survey was not
  run during the Oct–Nov 2025 shutdown; BLS never published that month). Last known value: 4.1 % (Aug 2026).
- **Queries (5, hand-picked, none dropped):** `unemployment`, `unemployment benefits`, `file for unemployment`,
  `unemployment office`, `unemployment claims`. Chosen to mirror Choi–Varian's *Welfare & Unemployment* /
  initial-claims category without any data-driven search (no `related_queries`). English, `geo="US"`,
  monthly (`gt.timeframe_for("M")` = 2004-01-01 → today), individual pulls (`gt.fetch_many`, mode
  `individual`). Non-zero share = 100 % for all five. GT covers 2004-01 → 2026-08 (Sept 2026 is partial
  and is dropped by the library), i.e. GT does **not** extend beyond the truth.
- **Alignment:** `truth.align(y, X, "M")` → 271 common months.
- **Horizons:** h=0 nowcast (`y_known_at_origin=False`: the latest month's rate is unknown, its GT is known)
  and h=1 (`y_known_at_origin=True`).
- **Window:** expanding; 120 rolling origins (targets 2016-09 → 2026-08); `min_train=60`.
- **Preprocessing:** `pp.raw_pipeline` (ZeroRepair → log1p), re-fitted inside every training window.
- **Target transform:** `transform="diff"` (model the change from the last known level).
- **Models:** GT model = `ols` ARX (3 AR lags in differences, 1 Fourier pair, GT lags 0–1 of the 5 terms
  = 10 GT regressors). Benchmarks = {`naive` (random walk), `seasonal_naive` (12), `ar_diff` (same design
  without GT)}. **Bench for each view = the lowest-RMSE target-only model in that view**, per the brief;
  that turned out to be `ar_diff` for the full-sample nowcast and `naive` everywhere else.
- **Views:** full sample and `exclude=("2020-03-01","2020-12-31")`.

## 5. Surprises / problems

1. **The full-sample RMSE win is one observation.** OLS+GT has *worse* MAE than naive even in the full
   sample (0.279 vs 0.256); only RMSE favours it because it got April 2020 half right (10.35 vs 14.8).
   Anyone who reports only RMSE + OOS R² on the full sample would conclude "GT cuts nowcast error by 52 %".
2. **Clark–West paradox again.** CW p=0.001 (h=0 excl.) and 0.010 (h=1 excl.) for a model whose OOS R² is
   −4. CW detects *some* signal net of estimation noise; it is not evidence of beating the benchmark. The
   verdict rule (R² > 0 **and** p < 0.10) handled it correctly, but the skill must never quote CW alone.
3. **Direction vs level.** The GT model has the best directional accuracy in every view (0.60–0.67 vs ≈0.5
   for AR, undefined for naive) while being much worse in levels: with 10 GT regressors and ~200 training
   rows, OLS overfits the level of the change but gets its sign more often than chance. A tightly
   regularised or sign-only use of GT might be a better Approach A than OLS.
4. **AR-in-differences is dangerous at h=1 with a known origin.** ar_diff full-sample h=1 RMSE is 2.21 vs
   naive 1.02: after the +10.3 pt April-2020 jump it extrapolated to 34 % for May 2020 (error −20.9). Even the
   "sensible" benchmark needs a shock guard; naive is the safer bench for this series at h ≥ 1.
5. **Missing month in the truth (library issue).** `UNRATE` has no 2025-10. `ev.naive`/`seasonal_naive`
   label targets positionally (`y.index[t+h]`) while `rolling_backtest` labels the target by calendar
   arithmetic but takes `y_true` positionally, so at origin 2025-09, h=1, the regression models are scored
   against the Nov-2025 value under a "2025-10" label and naive calls the same observation "2025-11";
   `compare` intersects the labels and silently drops one target (n=119 vs 120). Also one training row's
   "one-month" difference is really a two-month change. Impact here is negligible (1/120), but the library
   should either reindex to a complete `PeriodIndex` (leaving NaN and skipping those origins) or assert
   contiguity in `truth.align`.
6. **No true nowcast possible today.** GT for Sept 2026 is partial and dropped, so h=0 silently returned the
   h=1 forecast (the library notes it in `note`). A real nowcast would require pulling weekly/daily GT for
   the current month and aggregating — worth a follow-up in Approach C.
7. Nothing was slow: 6 s wall time, all but one pull cached.

## 7. Recommendation for the skill

For "what will the US unemployment rate be next month" with a Choi–Varian-style handful of raw keywords, the
skill should answer with the **random walk** (last published rate, ± the conformal band ≈ ±0.2 pt) and say
explicitly that Google Trends did not improve on it in a 10-year rolling backtest — neither for the
unpublished month nor one month ahead — and that the only period where searches helped was the March–April
2020 layoff shock. It must (a) benchmark against the best of {naive, AR-diff}, never AR-in-levels, and pick
naive as the bench at h ≥ 1 for persistent series; (b) show full-sample and shock-excluded tables side by
side and require OOS R² > 0 in **both** plus a DM/CW p < 0.10 before claiming a gain; (c) never quote
Clark–West on its own; (d) report MAE alongside RMSE so a one-month outlier cannot carry the story; and
(e) refuse the h=0 "nowcast" framing when GT does not actually extend past the last published truth month,
or fetch intra-month GT to make it real. Plain OLS on 10 raw GT regressors should not be the skill's GT model
for this question — if GT is used at all, it needs regularisation/smoothing (Approach C) and should be
presented as a directional hint (60–67 % sign accuracy) rather than a level forecast.
