# Experiment 02-Q2-C — US ILI % in 2 and 4 weeks, Approach C (Djorno-first ablation)

## 1. Setup

- **Question (Q2):** How high will US influenza-like-illness activity (ILI %) be in 2 and 4 weeks?
- **Truth:** `truth.fluview_ili("nat", 201040)` = CDC FluView ILINet national weighted ILI % via Delphi Epidata,
  weekly (Saturday week-end), 831 weeks 2010-10-09 → 2026-09-05, no gaps. Only the 260 weeks overlapping the GT
  window are used (Google's 5-year weekly limit). Sample mean 2.70 %, sd 1.56, min 0.87, max 8.30 (record peak
  week ending 2025-12-27).
- **Queries (17, pulled individually, `geo=US`, `timeframe="today 5-y"`, weekly 2021-09-12 → 2026-09-06):**
  flu, flu symptoms, influenza, flu a symptoms, fever, cough, sore throat, body aches, chills, tamiflu, flu medicine,
  flu test, how long does flu last, theraflu, nyquil, flu shot, stomach flu. Chosen as symptoms / treatments / tests,
  cross-checked against `gt.related_queries("flu", geo="US")` (top: "the flu", "flu shot", "flu symptoms", "flu test",
  "flu medicine", "flu a symptoms", "how long does flu last", "influenza"); "stomach flu" and "flu shot" were kept
  deliberately as known confounders for the clustering/detrending steps to deal with. Dropped by privacy-zero
  filter: 0. Failed pulls: 0. Zero share of every series: 0.00.
- **Alignment:** `truth.align(y, X, "W")` → common `W-SAT` PeriodIndex, 260 weeks 2021-09-18 → 2026-09-05,
  verified contiguous (every step = 1 week) and NaN-free, so no imputation was needed. The last complete Google week
  (Sun 2026-09-06 → Sat 2026-09-12) lies one week beyond the last published ILI week (2026-09-05), which is exactly
  the FluView publication lag; a non-intersected copy of X (`Xp`) keeps that week for the nowcast/forward forecast.
- **Horizons / information set:** h=0 nowcast, h=2, h=4, all with `y_known_at_origin=False` (at origin week t the GT
  for week t is known, ILI for week t is not yet published; AR lags start at y_{t-1}). Expanding window,
  `min_train=104` → 156 / 154 / 152 rolling origins, test period 2023-09 → 2026-09 (three flu seasons).
- **Design:** levels (no transform), p=4 AR lags, xlags=1, period=52, fourier_k=2.
- **Preprocessing (fitted inside each training window):**
  raw = ZeroRepair → log1p; Djorno = ZeroRepair → log1p → EWMA(halflife 1.5) → rolling 52-week detrend →
  hierarchical clustering (correlation distance, threshold 0.4). Ablation variants: `detrend=None`,
  `cluster=False`, and (added after seeing the first run) `halflife=0` (no smoothing) and "smooth only"
  (smooth, no detrend, no cluster) so that every step is isolated.
- **Models:** naive (h=0: y_{t-1}; h≥1 the library naive uses y_t, i.e. it is information-advantaged, so a
  `naive_lag1` = y_{t-1} row was added), seasonal_naive(52), AR(4)+Fourier; ridge (RidgeCV) with each pipeline;
  SARIMAX(2,0,0)+c with and without Djorno GT (exog x_{T-h}); step=1, all origins (fast enough: ≈20 s per horizon).
- **Benchmark rule:** best RMSE among target-only models *with the same information set*
  {naive_lag1/naive, seasonal_naive, ar, sarimax} → `ar` at h=0 and h=2, `seasonal_naive` at h=4.

| h=2 | gt_helps=True, best=ridge+raw, OOS R²=0.36 vs ar, CW p=0.000, DM p=0.014, **strong** | R²=0.31, CW 0.001, DM 0.074, strong |
| h=4 | gt_helps=True, best=ridge+raw, OOS R²=0.23 vs seasonal_naive, CW p=0.003, DM p=0.36, **moderate** | R²=0.24, CW 0.005, DM 0.39, moderate |

Honest reading: raw log-GT with ridge genuinely improves the ILI nowcast and the 2-week forecast (large R², both
tests significant, robust to removing the record peak), the 4-week gain is real on R² and Clark–West but not
Diebold–Mariano significant, and **the Djorno preprocessing chain — the hypothesis of this cell — makes every
horizon worse than raw GT** (full Djorno: R² 0.36 / 0.03 / −0.09), with the gain entirely confined to flu season.

### Ablation reading (RMSE, lower is better)

| step set | h=0 | h=2 | h=4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| raw (ZeroRepair+log) | **0.237** | **0.762** | **0.971** |
| + smooth only (EWMA 1.5) | 0.258 | 0.823 | 1.155 |
| + detrend + cluster, no smooth | 0.259 | 0.885 | 1.127 |
| + smooth + detrend (no cluster) | 0.273 | 0.912 | 1.256 |
| + smooth + cluster (no detrend) | 0.293 | 0.940 | 1.378 |
| full Djorno | 0.297 | 0.940 | 1.153 |
| SARIMAX(2,0,0) + full Djorno | 0.326 | 1.190 | 1.144 |

Every step costs accuracy on this weekly, fast-moving target. Smoothing with half-life 1.5 weeks introduces a
1–2-week lag on a signal whose whole value is its timeliness (worst at h=4). Clustering collapses 15 of 17 queries
into one factor and throws away the between-query information ridge could have weighted (worst at h=0/h=2).
Rolling detrending against a 52-week trailing mean removes the seasonal level, which for ILI *is* the signal. The
Djorno paper's benefit (seen in 00b for monthly unemployment in calm periods) does not transfer to weekly flu with a
17-query, strongly co-seasonal set; ridge's own shrinkage is enough regularisation here.

## 4. Forward forecast

Origin = week ending **2026-09-12** (GT known, ILI not yet published); last published ILI = **1.51 %** (week ending
2026-09-05, rising from 0.87 % in early August). Best configuration per horizon = ridge + raw GT (levels, p=4,
xlags=1, Fourier k=2, expanding). 90 % conformal half-width from the last 52 backtest residuals; the benchmark's own
forecast is shown because the benchmark is the better model off-season (Section 2).

| horizon | target week ending | ridge+raw point | 90 % interval | benchmark point (90 %) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| h=0 nowcast | 2026-09-12 | **1.77 %** | [1.38, 2.16] | ar 1.63 % [1.05, 2.22] |
| h=2 | 2026-09-26 | **2.05 %** | [0.88, 3.22] | ar 1.99 % [0.44, 3.55] |
| h=4 | 2026-10-10 | **2.49 %** | [0.59, 4.38] | seasonal_naive 1.64 % [0.08, 3.19] |

Calm-state intervals (residuals from origins whose last published ILI was < 2.5 %, n≈90): ±0.25 (h=0), ±0.71 (h=2),
±1.34 (h=4) — i.e. the season-wide conformal widths above are conservative for a September origin.

Drivers (standardised ridge coefficients at the origin): h=0 is dominated by `y_lag1` (+1.38) with GT adding
`how long does flu last` (+0.56), `flu shot` (−0.43, the September vaccine wave is a negative signal for ILI),
`tamiflu` (l1 −0.42 / l0 +0.37) and `flu a symptoms`. At h=2 and h=4 the AR lags recede and the GT terms
`tamiflu__l0` (+0.88 / +0.98), `cough` (l1 +0.54 / +0.81), `flu symptoms`, `theraflu`, plus the Fourier terms carry
the forecast; the h=4 point of 2.49 % is well above last year's 1.64 % for the same week and rests mainly on
`tamiflu` and `cough` — given the off-season RMSE of 0.61 vs 0.24 for seasonal-naive, the honest 4-week answer is
"about 1.6–2.5 %, probably rising, with the benchmark the safer point".

## 5. Surprises / problems

1. **Leakage bug found and fixed in `ev.rolling_backtest`** (`src/trends_predict/evaluate.py`, training-row
   selection). Training rows were `target_date <= origin`; for h=0 (and any `y_known_at_origin=False` run) that
   includes the origin row itself — the model was trained on the very value it was nowcasting, and at h≥1 on one
   not-yet-published week. Probe before the fix: ridge+djorno h=0 RMSE **0.246**; after the fix **0.297** (the leak
   flattered the nowcast by ~17 % RMSE). Fixed to `target_date <= last known y` (= origin−1 under publication lag).
   **All h=0 / publication-lag results produced before this fix (incl. 00b smoke test h=0 rows) are optimistic.**
2. **Library extension:** `ev.rolling_sarimax(..., y_known_at_origin=False)` added (fit through t−1, forecast h+1
   steps with exog x_{t−h}..x_t) so SARIMAX shares the ridge/AR information set. A concurrent agent added the
   matching publication-lag origin to `ev.forecast_next` (h≥1, `y_known_at_origin=False`, X extending beyond y)
   while this cell ran; this cell relies on it.
3. The requested exclusion window (2020-03 → 2021-06) is outside the sample; replaced by the record 2025-26 peak as
   the supplementary robustness view (Section 2).
4. `ev.naive` at h≥1 assumes y_t is known; under publication lag it is information-advantaged (RMSE 0.85 vs 1.09 for
   the honest lag-1 random walk at h=2). The skill should use the lag-consistent random walk as a benchmark when
   `y_known_at_origin=False`.
5. `compare()`'s `DirAcc` at h≥1 uses y_t as the reference level, which is not known under the lag — column is
   meaningless for those rows.
6. Clustering threshold 0.4 is far too aggressive for co-seasonal flu queries (15/17 in one cluster); no sensitivity
   run on the threshold was done (budget/time), so "clustering hurts" is specific to that default.
7. No data problems: 0 privacy zeros, aligned index contiguous, GT extends exactly one week past FluView.

## 7. Recommendation for the skill

For weekly epidemiological targets like ILI the skill should (a) frame the answer as *nowcast + short horizon*
(h=0 and h=2 have strong, test-significant gains; h=4 only weakly), (b) use **raw log GT + ridge** as the default GT
model and treat the Djorno chain (EWMA smoothing, rolling detrend, correlation clustering) as an *option to test*,
not a default — on this target every step degraded accuracy, and smoothing is actively harmful at h≥2; (c) always
run the same publication-lag information set for every model (lag-consistent naive, `y_known_at_origin=False` for
SARIMAX too) and benchmark against the best of {naive_lag, seasonal_naive, AR}; (d) report a **regime split**: GT
gains are entirely in-season (Oct–Mar) and the target-only benchmark is 2× better off-season, so an off-season
question (like today's) should present the AR/seasonal-naive number as primary or at least alongside the GT point,
with intervals from calm-state residuals; (e) caveat that 5-year weekly GT gives only ~3 test seasons and that the
2020–21 exclusion cannot even be evaluated; and (f) refuse to over-claim beyond ~4 weeks, where DM is insignificant
and the GT point (2.49 %) diverges from the seasonal benchmark (1.64 %) on the strength of two queries.
