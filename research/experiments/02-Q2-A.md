# 02-Q2-A — US ILI % in 2 and 4 weeks × Approach A (Choi–Varian minimal)

Date: 2026-09-17 · Script: `research/experiments/scripts/02-Q2-A.py` · JSON: `02-Q2-A.json` ·
GT requests: 11 (5 primary + 5 chained extension + 1 duplicate vintage of `flu symptoms` created during a 429 retry; all cached after) · Wall time ≈ 3 min incl. fetches, 26–31 s cached.

## 1. Setup

- **Question (Q2).** How high will US influenza-like-illness activity (weighted ILI %, ILINet) be in 2 and 4 weeks?
  Plus the h=0 nowcast of the week whose ILI has not yet been published.
- **Truth.** `truth.fluview_ili("nat", 201040)` — CDC FluView ILINet national wILI via Delphi Epidata, weekly,
  index = Saturday week-end. 2010-10-09 → 2026-09-05, 831 weeks, **0 missing Saturdays, 0 NaN** (national ILINet has
  been reported year-round over this range, so the "off-season gaps in older years" caveat did not bite; nothing had to
  be filled). Latest print: week ending 2026-09-05 = 1.509 %.
- **Queries (hand-picked, 5).** `flu symptoms`, `flu`, `tamiflu`, `fever`, `cough`. Weekly via
  `gt.fetch_many(kws, geo="US", timeframe="today 5-y")`, individual pulls (each 0–100 on its own scale). Window
  2021-09-12 → 2026-09-06 (Sunday-start weeks), 261 rows. 0 dropped by the privacy-zero filter, 0 zeros, 0 NaN.
- **Alignment.** `truth.align(y, X, "W")` → W-SAT periods; Google's Sunday-start week and CDC's Saturday-end week for
  the same 7 days map to one period. Aligned frame: 2021-09-12/18 → 2026-08-30/09-05, **260 weeks, 0 gaps, 0 NaN**
  (asserted in the script). GT has exactly one week beyond the last ILI print (period 2026-09-06/09-12), which is the
  h=0 nowcast target.
- **Horizons.** h=0 nowcast, h=2, h=4 — all with `y_known_at_origin=False` (at origin week t the latest published ILI is
  y_{t-1}; GT is known through t). Targets y_t, y_{t+2}, y_{t+4}.
- **Window / design.** Expanding window, `min_train=104`, p=4 AR lags, `xlags=1`, `period=52`, `fourier_k=2`;
  156 / 154 / 152 rolling origins (2023-09 → 2026-09, i.e. three complete seasons 2023-24, 2024-25, 2025-26 in the test set).
- **Preprocessing.** `pp.raw_pipeline` (ZeroRepair → log1p), fitted inside each training window.
- **Models.** GT: `ols` ARX in **levels** and with `transform="diff"`. Target-only set: `naive_lag` (random walk from the
  last *published* value y_{t-1}; for h=0 this is `ev.naive`), `seasonal_naive` (period 52), `ar_levels`, `ar_diff`.
  `ev.naive` at h≥1 predicts with y_t, which is **not known** at the origin under the publication lag, so it is shown
  only as an italic reference row ("naive_y_t (not known at origin)") and never used as benchmark.
  **Benchmark = best information-consistent target-only model by RMSE** on the common sample (chosen per horizon).
- **Exclusion view.** `exclude=("2020-03-01","2021-06-30")`. On the primary `today 5-y` sample this is **vacuous**
  (the sample starts 2021-09), so the "excl" tables equal the full tables. To make the COVID exclusion meaningful I
  added a clearly-labelled **secondary chained sample**: the same 5 keywords over `2017-01-01 2021-12-31` (5 more
  requests, weekly), rescaled per keyword to the primary window by the ratio of means over the 16 overlapping weeks
  (scale factors 2.18 / 2.21 / 1.88 / 1.05 / 1.05; overlap correlations 0.993–1.000), then spliced. 505 aligned weeks,
  0 gaps; ~400 origins (2019-01 → 2026-09), of which 70 fall in the exclusion window.
- **Library extensions (documented, not patched into `src/`).** (a) `naive_lag` as above. (b) `forecast_pub_lag`, a
  forward forecaster that puts the origin at the latest GT week for any h when `y_known_at_origin=False` (the h=0
  origin logic generalised). While this cell was running, `ev.forecast_next` was updated in the library with exactly
  that behaviour and `ev.rolling_backtest` received a fix that stops training on rows whose target is not yet published
  under the lag; the results below were produced with the fixed library, and `forecast_next` and my helper return
  identical points to 16 digits (kept as a cross-check).

## 3. Verdict

`ev.verdict(tab, bench, gt_models=["ols+GT_levels","ols+GT_diff"])`, primary sample (full = excl):

- **h=0:** `gt_helps=True, strength=strong` — "ols+GT_levels lowers squared error by 56% vs ar_levels (Clark–West p=0.000)"; DM p=0.012.
- **h=2:** `gt_helps=True, strength=strong` — "ols+GT_levels lowers squared error by 36% vs ar_levels (Clark–West p=0.000)"; DM p=0.018.
- **h=4:** `gt_helps=False, strength=none` — "best GT model ols+GT_diff has OOS R²=−0.13 vs seasonal_naive (Clark–West p=0.00); not a reliable gain"; DM p=0.62.

Chained sample: h=0 strong (R² 0.48 full / 0.51 excl, DM p ≤ 0.001); h=2 strong (0.43 / 0.38, DM p ≤ 0.002);
h=4 `gt_helps=True` by the rule (R² 0.25 / 0.19, CW p < 1e-4) but DM p = 0.10 / 0.20, i.e. the point gain at h=4 is
real on average but not significant by the non-nested test, and it vanishes on the shorter primary sample.

**Honest reading.** Five raw flu keywords in a plain OLS ARX give a large, stable, significant improvement for the
*nowcast* and the *2-week* forecast (roughly a third to a half of the AR benchmark's squared error, robust to dropping
COVID and to doubling the test period), but the gain is entirely an in-season, turning-point phenomenon — the same
model is *worse* than the benchmark in the off-season, and at 4 weeks the off-season damage (negative ILI predictions
from an unregularised levels model) wipes out the in-season gain, so h=4 is a coin flip against seasonal-naive.

## 5. Surprises / problems

1. **`fever` is not a flu keyword in the US.** Its 5-year maximum (100) is the week of 2026-08-16, and its seven
   highest weeks are all in May–September (2024-06-30, 2024-08-25, 2024-09-01, 2025-05-18, 2026-06-07/14, 2026-07-12,
   2026-08-16), while at the actual flu peaks (Jan–Feb 2025, Dec 2025) it only reaches 62–63. Google's max-normalisation
   then compresses its in-season resolution. It should be dropped or replaced (e.g. `flu test`, `influenza`) by any
   keyword-screening step; OLS gave it small coefficients here, so it did not wreck the result, but it is a live
   example of why hand-picked terms need a seasonality/relevance check. The other four peak where they should
   (`flu symptoms`/`flu` 2025-12-28, `tamiflu` 2025-12-21, `cough` 2024-12-22).
2. **Levels OLS produces negative ILI.** At h=4, 8 of 152 GT forecasts were < 0 and off-season RMSE quadrupled
   (0.29 → 1.13). A log target (or at least a floor at 0 and a regularised estimator) is a must for ILI.
3. **Higher MAPE, lower RMSE.** Every GT model has *worse* MAPE than its benchmark while having better RMSE: the
   gain is at peaks (large absolute errors), the loss is in low-ILI weeks (large relative errors). Report both, or
   split in-season/off-season, or the headline misleads.
4. **The mandated COVID exclusion is vacuous with `today 5-y`.** Any weekly cell that wants a 2020–21 robustness
   view needs the chained-window construction (documented above; 16-week overlap, corr ≥ 0.993, scale factors up to
   2.2 because the 2017–21 window's max was the March-2020 spike). With it, the verdicts did not change.
5. **`ev.naive` at h≥1 is information-inconsistent under publication lag** (uses y_t); it is 0.2–0.3 RMSE better than
   the fair `naive_lag` and would have masked part of the GT gain had it been used as bench. Suggest a
   `y_known_at_origin` flag on `ev.naive`.
6. **Concurrent library edits during the run.** `ev.rolling_backtest` (training-row leak fix for `y_known_at_origin=False`)
   and `ev.forecast_next` (publication-lag origin for h≥1) were modified while this cell ran; the final results were
   regenerated with the updated code (bit-identical to the previous run, so the fix did not alter this cell's numbers).
   `len(gt.catalog())` deltas are unusable for budget counting when cells run in parallel; the script counts its own
   keyword/timeframe vintages instead.
7. Google returned 429s during the first pulls (handled by the library's backoff; ~65 s for 5 keywords).
8. **Only three test seasons** on the primary sample (min_train=104 eats two of the five years). Flu verdicts on
   `today 5-y` rest on ~66 in-season weeks; the chained sample (seven seasons) is the more trustworthy view.

## 7. Recommendation for the skill

For weekly epidemiological targets like ILI the skill should (i) frame the deliverable as **nowcast + 1–2 week
forecast** and say plainly that at 4 weeks a seasonal or AR benchmark is as good as anything GT offers with a minimal
keyword set; (ii) use the publication-lag protocol throughout (`y_known_at_origin=False`, `naive_lag` as the random-walk
bench, origin at the latest GT week for the forward forecast); (iii) pick the benchmark per horizon from
{naive_lag, seasonal_naive, ar_levels, ar_diff} — for ILI the AR in levels wins at h≤2 and seasonal-naive at h=4, and
`transform="diff"` is *not* the right default for a mean-reverting seasonal series (it lost to levels at every horizon);
(iv) model a **log or floored target** and prefer ridge/LASSO over plain OLS once xlags×keywords exceeds ~10 columns,
because the off-season blow-ups are what kill h=4; (v) screen hand-picked keywords for seasonality that matches the
target (drop `fever`-type terms whose peak is in summer) and report in-season vs off-season error separately alongside
RMSE and MAPE; (vi) when the user wants a shock-exclusion view on a weekly series, either build the chained multi-window
GT series or state that the 5-year window contains no such period — never print an "excluded" table that is identical
to the full one without saying so.
