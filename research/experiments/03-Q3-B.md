# Experiment 03-Q3-B — "Will the S&P 500 be higher one month from now?" · Approach B (ARGO-style breadth, weekly, related-query expansion)

Date: 2026-09-17 · Script: `research/experiments/scripts/03-Q3-B.py` · JSON: `research/experiments/03-Q3-B.json`
GT requests: 34 (5 `related_queries` + 29 individual weekly pulls); wall time 2,423 s (40 min, of which ~27 min is ElasticNetCV).

Follow-up to `03-Q3-A` (8 hand-picked monthly terms, ridge/OLS: no edge). This cell tests the two things
03-Q3-A recommended: **weekly** frequency and **related-query expansion** with sparse (LASSO/ENet) models,
expanding and sliding windows, against the new `ev.drift` benchmark.

## 1. Setup

- **Question.** Direction of the S&P 500 over the next month, cast as the 4-week-ahead log change of the
  weekly close (`h=4`, `transform="diff"`, `y_known_at_origin=True`). Primary metric: direction hit-rate
  vs the always-up base rate; secondary: OOS R² vs the best target-only model.
- **Truth.** `truth.yahoo("^GSPC", freq="W")` — Yahoo Finance weekly last close (= Friday close), 1,185
  weeks 2004-01 → 2026-09-13; the current partial week is dropped by the library (verified). `y = log(close)`.
  **Alignment fix (see §5.1):** `truth.yahoo(freq="W")` labels each Friday close with the *following Sunday*,
  and `truth.align(…, "W")` maps a Sunday to the Sat-ending week that *starts* on it. As specified, the
  "origin" GT week would therefore be the week *after* the close — a one-week look-ahead. The price labels
  were shifted back one day (Sunday → Saturday) so each close sits in its own Sun–Sat week; the
  as-specified alignment was run once as a leakage-sensitivity check (§2d).
- **Queries.** Seeds `stock market`, `recession`, `stock market crash`, `S&P 500`, `sell stocks` →
  `gt.expand_queries(seeds, geo="US", timeframe="today 5-y", max_per_seed=10)` → **52 candidates → 29 kept,
  23 dropped** (drop list hard-coded in the script with a reason each):
  - irrelevant: `gum recession`;
  - historical / homework (school-calendar driven): `stock market crash 1929`, `great stock market crash`,
    `great depression stock market crash`, `great depression`, `stock market crash of 1929`,
    `the great depression stock market crash`, `the great depression`, `great recession`;
  - definitional: `what is the stock market`, `what is recession`, `what is s&p 500`, `what are stocks`;
  - generic / ambiguous tokens: `stock`, `etf`, `dow` (Dow Inc.; `dow jones` kept);
  - near-duplicates of a seed: `the stock market`, `today stock market`, `the recession`,
    `the stock market crash`, `stock market dow`, `s&p 500 stock`, `s&p 500 price`.
  - **Kept (29):** stock market, market news, stock market news, stock market open, us stock market,
    the stock market today, stock market crash, did the stock market crash, recession, what is a recession,
    us recession, recession definition, economy recession, economy, economic recession, S&P 500,
    s&p 500 index, s&p 500 today, s&p 500 futures, nasdaq, dow jones, sell stocks, stocks to sell,
    buy sell stocks, how to sell stocks, stocks to buy, buy and sell stocks, when to sell stocks,
    buy or sell stocks. (`what is a recession` / `recession definition` were kept deliberately: they
    spike in recession scares, e.g. 2022, and are FEARS-type terms rather than homework.)
  - Individual weekly pulls, geo=US, `today 5-y`: 261 weeks 2021-09-12 → 2026-09-06, none dropped by the
    privacy-zero filter (min non-zero share 1.0), none failed. Rising queries were not used (they are
    dominated by ticker noise — `meta stock`, `djt stock` — and homework text).
- **Aligned sample.** 261 weeks (2021-09-12/18 → 2026-09-06/12). `min_train=104`, `h=4` → **153 rolling
  origins**, targets 2023-10-08/14 → 2026-09-06/12. Note this evaluation window is a bull market
  (71 % of 4-week windows up).
- **Design.** p = 2 AR lags of the weekly log return, `xlags=1` (58 GT columns: 29 terms × lags 0–1),
  `fourier_k=0`, `period=52`.
- **Preprocessing (fitted inside each window):** `raw` = ZeroRepair → log1p; `diffGT` = ZeroRepair → log1p →
  first difference (GT in changes).
- **Models.** Target-only: `naive` (zero change), `drift` (`ev.drift`: random walk + expanding-mean weekly
  drift), `ar_diff` (AR(2) on returns). GT: `lasso`, `enet` × {raw, diffGT} × {expanding, sliding
  `window=104`} = 8. Bench = lowest-MAE target-only model per view (= `drift` in every view).
- **Views.** Full sample; `exclude=("2020-02-01","2020-06-30")` as mandated — **vacuous** here because the
  5-year weekly sample starts in 2021-09 (identical table); plus an extra shock view
  `exclude=("2025-03-01","2025-05-31")` (the April-2025 tariff crash and rebound, the only large shock
  in the sample).
- **Inference caveat (overlap).** With h = 4 on weekly data, consecutive 4-week targets share 3 weeks, so
  forecast errors are MA(3)-autocorrelated. DM and CW use a Bartlett HAC variance with lag h−1 = 3,
  which handles this; the binomial direction tests do **not** (their n = 153 overstates the ~38
  independent 4-week windows) and should be read as anti-conservative.

## 3. Verdict

- **Full sample (h = 4):** `{'gt_helps': False, 'best_model': 'lasso+diffGT+exp', 'oos_r2': -0.001,
  'cw_p': 0.568, 'dm_p': 0.838, 'strength': 'none'}` — "best GT model lasso+diffGT+exp has OOS R²=−0.00
  vs drift (Clark–West p=0.57); not a reliable gain".
- **Excl. 2020-02…06 (mandated):** identical to the full sample (vacuous exclusion) — `gt_helps: False`.
- **Excl. tariff shock 2025-03…05 (extra):** `{'gt_helps': True, 'best_model': 'lasso+raw+w104',
  'oos_r2': 0.043, 'cw_p': 0.012, 'dm_p': 0.235, 'strength': 'moderate'}`.

**Honest reading:** breadth and weekly frequency did not change the null result of 03-Q3-A. On the
full sample no GT model beats the random walk with drift on squared error (best OOS R² −0.001; the
"best" GT model is one whose LASSO selected *zero* search terms at every single origin, i.e. it is the
drift with shrunk AR lags), and on direction every model — GT, AR or drift — loses to the trivial rule
"the index is higher in four weeks" (71.2 % hit-rate); the GT models call "up" 90–95 % of the time and
merely inherit the drift (`p_vs_always_up` ≥ 0.15, and those binomial p-values are already too
generous because of the overlap). The one "moderate" verdict appears only after removing the largest
shock in the sample post hoc, only for the sliding-window models, with a 4 % MSE reduction, CW p 0.01
but DM p 0.24, on ~35 effectively independent 4-week windows — and even there the same models are
*worse* than the drift on direction (69.3 % vs 71.4 %, always-up 74.3 %). Excluding the shock helps the
GT models precisely because they lost to the drift *during* the April-2025 crash — the moment a fear
index would have been expected to earn its keep. Google Trends provides **no validated edge** for the
one-month direction of the S&P 500 with this design either.

## 4. Forward forecast

- **Winning configuration:** `drift` (best target-only model; no GT model qualified on the full sample).
- Origin: week 2026-09-06/12, Friday 2026-09-11 close **7,656.98**. Target: week 2026-10-04/10, i.e. the
  Friday 2026-10-09 close (4 weeks after the origin close; ~3 weeks after today, 2026-09-17).
- Point: 4-week log change +0.0084 (weekly drift 0.0021 × 4) → **7,722 (+0.84 %)**.
  90 % conformal interval (recent-52 residuals, half-width 0.0747 in log terms):
  **7,166 … 8,321 (−6.4 % … +8.7 %)**. Rolling empirical coverage of this rule over the backtest: 88 %
  (nominal 90 %); with all-origin residuals the half-width is 0.066.
- **"Probability the index is higher":** not model-based. The defensible number is the base rate of
  up 4-week windows: 63.8 % over the 5-year weekly sample (2021-09 → 2026-09), 71.2 % over the
  evaluation window (2023-10 → 2026-09, a bull market). Google Trends does not move it.
- GT reference (not the headline): `lasso+raw+w104` (the "moderate" model of view 2c) → 7,691
  (+0.45 %), interval 7,152 … 8,271, driven by `s&p 500 futures_l0` (+0.0059), `dow jones_l1` (+0.0047),
  `how to sell stocks_l0` (−0.0010), `what is a recession_l1` (−0.0002), `stock market crash_l1`
  (−0.0001); `lasso+diffGT+exp` (best on the full sample) selected no GT feature and returns the drift
  (7,723, +0.87 %). Both point the same way as the drift and lie well inside its interval.

## 5. Surprises / problems

1. **Week-alignment look-ahead in the specified recipe.** `truth.yahoo(freq="W")` resamples with
   `W-SUN` and labels the Friday close with the *following* Sunday; `truth.align(…, "W")` converts a
   Sunday to the `W-SAT` period that *starts* on it, which is the same period a Google Sunday-dated row
   maps to. Net effect as specified: the "origin" GT row is the week *after* the origin close, and the
   final Friday close (2026-09-11) is dropped from the aligned sample because its partner GT week is
   still partial. Fixed by shifting price labels back one day; `truth.align` should grow a `how="last"` /
   "period of the observation date" option (or `truth.yahoo` should label weeks by their Saturday) so the
   skill does this automatically for stock-like series. The as-specified alignment was run as a check
   (§2d) and, reassuringly, did not manufacture an edge either.
2. **The mandated exclusion window is empty** for any `today 5-y` weekly pull made after mid-2025; the
   skill should detect an exclusion that removes zero targets and say so, or pick a shock inside the
   sample. The extra tariff-crash view was added for that reason.
3. **Post-hoc exclusion can flip the verdict** (`none` → `moderate`) even when the full-sample result
   is a clean null and the direction metrics do not improve. `ev.verdict` relies on CW *or* DM; here CW
   is significant while DM is not, on a 4 % MSE gain with ~35 effective observations. The skill should
   only promote a GT model if the verdict holds on the full sample *and* the exclusion view (the script's
   `winner()` already requires both), and should report the direction tables alongside.
4. **LASSO honesty.** The best full-sample GT model selected no search term at any of 153 origins; the
   expanding raw-GT model selected a term at 11 % of origins. Breadth (58 GT columns) did not give the
   sparse learners anything to hold onto; the sliding-window models pick a different handful each week.
5. **Rate limiting.** pytrends reported repeated 429s (the library's backoff succeeded; 0 failed pulls).
   29 weekly pulls took roughly 10 minutes.
6. **ElasticNetCV is slow**: 4 l1-ratios × 30 alphas × time-series CV at each of 153 origins took 4–11
   min per backtest (ENet total ≈ 27 min of the 40 min wall time) and produced results identical to
   LASSO in three of four configurations. The skill should default to LASSO and treat ENet as optional.
7. **Binomial direction tests overstate n** under overlapping 4-week targets (MA(3) errors); DM/CW use
   HAC lag 3 and are fine. All "p < 0.001 vs 0.5" values again come from models that say "up" ≥ 90 %
   of the time.
8. Sample is a single bull-market regime (2023-10 → 2026-09) with one shock; a 5-year weekly window
   cannot contain 2020. A longer weekly history would need stitched 5-year windows (2 × 29 pulls, over
   budget here) with cross-window rescaling.

## 7. Recommendation for the skill

For "will index X be higher in a month" questions the answer is now the same at monthly (03-Q3-A) and
weekly (this cell) frequency, with hand-picked and with related-query-expanded terms, with ridge and
with sparse learners, expanding and sliding: nothing beats random-walk-with-drift on size, nothing
beats "always up" on sign, and the sparse models say so themselves by selecting nothing. The skill
should (i) answer with the unconditional base rate and the drift point + conformal interval, (ii)
state plainly that Google Trends adds no validated information and refuse to emit a search-conditioned
probability, (iii) require `gt_helps` on *both* the full sample and the exclusion view before ever
using a GT model — a verdict that only appears after a post-hoc shock exclusion is a red flag, not a
finding, especially when the excluded period is the one where a fear index should have helped —
(iv) show the direction hit-rate against the always-up base rate and warn that overlapping multi-step
targets inflate the binomial n, and (v) fix weekly alignment for price series (close dated inside its
own week) and flag exclusion windows that fall outside the sample. Further Q3 cells are not worth
their budget unless the design changes qualitatively (e.g. daily frequency with intra-week
publication timing, or a volatility/drawdown target rather than direction).
