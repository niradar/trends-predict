# Experiment 03-Q3-A — "Will the S&P 500 be higher one month from now?" · Approach A (minimal predictive regression)

Date: 2026-09-17 · Script: `research/experiments/scripts/03-Q3-A.py` · JSON: `research/experiments/03-Q3-A.json`
GT requests: 7 (first run, 22 s); final rerun fully cached (0 requests, 11 s).

## 1. Setup

- **Question.** Direction of the S&P 500 over the next month. Bulut (2018) found Google data helps
  direction, not magnitude, and only for some assets — so the primary metric here is DirAcc (hit-rate).
- **Truth.** `truth.yahoo("^GSPC", freq="M")` — Yahoo Finance monthly *last close*, 2004-01 → 2026-08
  (272 obs after dropping the incomplete current month), modelled as `y = log(close)`.
  The target is the one-month log return via `transform="diff"`. **Partial-month fix:** Yahoo's
  2026-09 row is a mid-month close (as of the 16th/17th); both `y` and `X` were truncated to months
  `< 2026-09` so the last observation is a genuine month-end value.
- **Queries (8, hand-picked, geo=US, monthly `2004-01-01 … today`, individual pulls, none dropped;
  min non-zero share 0.993):** `stock market`, `recession`, `market crash`, `buy stocks`,
  `sell stocks`, `inflation`, `gold price`, `unemployment`.
  Justification: attention/fear terms of the Preis–Moat–Stanley (2013) and Da–Engelberg–Gao FEARS
  type (`stock market`, `market crash`, `recession`), explicit trading-intent terms (`buy stocks`,
  `sell stocks`), the two macro risks investors search for (`inflation`, `unemployment` — the latter
  already cached from cell 01, hence 7 requests), and a safe-haven proxy (`gold price`).
- **Horizon / origin.** h = 1 month, `y_known_at_origin=True` (last month-end close known),
  expanding window, `min_train=60` → 211 rolling origins, targets 2009-02 → 2026-08.
- **Design.** p = 2 AR lags of the log return, `xlags=1` (GT at t and t−1), `period=12`,
  `fourier_k=0`, `transform="diff"`.
- **Preprocessing (fitted inside each training window):** `raw` = ZeroRepair → log1p;
  `djorno` = `pp.djorno_pipeline(period=12, halflife=1.0)` (log → EWMA → rolling detrend →
  clustering); `diffGT` = ZeroRepair → log1p → first difference (GT in changes).
- **Models.** Benchmarks (target-only): `naive` (zero expected return = random walk),
  `rw_drift` (random walk + expanding-mean drift — **added**, see §5), `seasonal_naive`, `ar_diff`.
  GT models: `ols` and `ridge` × {raw, djorno, diffGT} = 6.
  Bench for `ev.compare` = lowest-RMSE target-only model in each view (`rw_drift` in both).
- **Views.** Full sample and `exclude=("2020-02-01","2020-06-30")` (COVID crash + rebound).
- **Extra direction metrics.** Hit-rate of sign(predicted return) vs sign(realised return),
  `scipy.stats.binomtest` two-sided vs 0.5 and vs the "always up" base rate, share of months the
  model predicted "up".

## 3. Verdict

- **Full sample:** `{'gt_helps': False, 'best_model': 'ridge+diffGT', 'oos_r2': -0.011, 'cw_p': 0.430,
  'dm_p': 0.594, 'strength': 'none'}`
- **Excl. 2020-02..06:** `{'gt_helps': False, 'best_model': 'ridge+diffGT', 'oos_r2': -0.003, 'cw_p': 0.229,
  'dm_p': 0.842, 'strength': 'none'}`

**Honest reading:** no GT configuration beats a random walk with drift on squared error, and no
model — GT, AR, or drift — beats the trivial rule "the index is higher next month" (66.4 % hit-rate)
on direction; every hit-rate that is "significant vs 0.5" belongs to a model that predicts "up"
82–90 % of the time and is simply inheriting the equity drift (all `p_vs_always_up` ≥ 0.10, and
the models that trade direction more actively — OLS variants, 52–58 % up-calls — fall to 52–55 %
hit-rates, indistinguishable from a coin flip). Google Trends provides no usable edge for the
one-month direction of the S&P 500 with this design.

## 4. Forward forecast

- **Winning configuration:** `rw_drift` (best target-only model; no GT model qualified in either view).
- Origin 2026-08 (month-end close 7,686.14); target = 2026-09 month-end close.
- Point: log-return +0.0071 → **7,741** (+0.71 %). 90 % conformal interval (recent-36 residuals,
  half-width 0.0658 in log terms): **7,248 … 8,267** (−5.7 % … +7.6 %). Rolling empirical coverage
  of this rule over the backtest: 90.9 % (nominal 90 %).
- **"Probability the index is higher":** the backtest does *not* justify a model-based probability.
  The only defensible number is the unconditional base rate: 64.6 % of months since 2004 (67.5 % of
  the last 120) closed higher than the previous month. Google Trends does not move that number.
- GT reference (not the headline): ridge+diffGT → 7,737 (+0.66 %), interval 7,266 … 8,238; the twelve
  largest standardised coefficients are all ~±0.001 (`inflation_l1` +, `stock market_l1` +,
  `buy stocks_l1` +, `recession_l1` −, `market crash_l0` −, `sell stocks_l0` −, …) — i.e. ridge
  shrank the GT block to almost nothing and kept the intercept.
- Caveat on timing: we are mid-September, so "one month from now" in calendar terms is mid-October;
  the monthly design can only forecast the September month-end close from the August close.

## 5. Surprises / problems

1. **Benchmark artefact, again (cf. 00b).** With plain `naive` (zero return) as bench, the
   excluded-shock view produced `gt_helps=True, strength="moderate"` (OOS R² 0.024, CW p 0.012)
   for ridge+diffGT. Adding a random-walk-with-drift benchmark (`rw_drift`, expanding-mean return —
   implemented in the script as a 10-line `BacktestResult` builder, not yet in `evaluate.py`)
   turned that into OOS R² −0.003, CW p 0.23. For a drifting series the zero-return naive is too
   weak a bench in exactly the way AR-in-levels was too weak for unemployment.
2. **Hit-rate vs 0.5 is the wrong null for equities.** Every "p < 0.001 vs 0.5" in the direction
   tables comes from a model that says "up" ≥ 82 % of the time. The right null is the always-up
   base rate (66 %), against which nothing is significant.
3. **Partial current month.** `truth.yahoo(..., freq="M")` returns the current month with its
   latest close, and the cached monthly GT frames contain a 2026-09 row; both were truncated to
   `< 2026-09`. Without this the last origin would have been a 17-day "month" — a subtle leakage
   /partial-period bug the skill must guard against for any month-start-indexed price series.
4. **Concurrent catalog writes.** `len(gt.catalog())` deltas over-counted my requests (17 vs the
   true 7) because other cells were pulling simultaneously; the script now counts catalog rows by
   its own keywords.
5. Seasonal-naive is catastrophically bad for a price level (MASE 1.03, OOS R² −16): the
   benchmark set should drop it for non-seasonal persistent series rather than report it.
6. OLS with 16 GT regressors + 2 AR lags is significantly *worse* than the drift (DM p 0.002 for
   ols+raw): estimation noise on ~150–270 monthly observations. Ridge is the right family here but
   it wins only by shrinking GT to zero.

## 7. Recommendation for the skill

For "will asset X be higher in a month" questions the skill should (i) model log returns with
`transform="diff"`, (ii) put **random walk with drift** — not just zero-return naive — in the
target-only benchmark set (promote the script's `rw_drift` into `evaluate.py`), (iii) report the
direction hit-rate against the *always-up base rate* as well as against 0.5, and (iv) truncate
month-start-indexed price series to complete months before aligning. With those in place the
verdict here is unambiguous: 8 sensible search terms, three preprocessings and two regressors give
no out-of-sample edge on either the size or the sign of the next monthly S&P 500 move. The skill
should answer such questions with the unconditional base rate and the drift interval, state plainly
that Google Trends does not improve on them, and refuse to emit a search-conditioned probability.
It may caveat that the literature's positive direction results (Bulut 2018, Preis et al. 2013) are
for weekly/daily horizons, specific assets or trading strategies with look-ahead-sensitive term
selection — if a follow-up cell is wanted, it should be weekly frequency with the `related_queries`
expansion rather than more monthly hand-picked terms.
